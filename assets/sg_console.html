Security Group Console

Every account in the database

The database is the source of truth for security groups: every group and rule, as wanted and as AWS last held it. Changes are change requests: one person proposes, a different person approves, then they are applied. Changes made in AWS outside the tool are found by Refresh from AWS. An account in surveillance mode is only reported on: what to change, and change requests, but nothing is applied. In enforce mode approved changes can be applied, once a permission check (DryRun, nothing changes) shows this identity can make them.

AWS sign-in

How do you sign in?

Signs in through your organisation's AWS access portal (IAM Identity Center), the same as aws sso login. You approve it on AWS's own page; the sign-in stays on this computer. Nothing is changed in AWS: the console reads until two people approve a change in an account switched to enforce mode.

Uses a profile or access key in ~/.aws on this computer: it acts as that identity. In other accounts it assumes one role you name (OrganizationAccountAccessRole unless you choose another); the organisation's accounts and OUs are read with the profile, so use the management account or a delegated administrator to see them all. Keys never leave this computer.

Read your accounts

Reading an account puts its security groups into the console and reads its VPC flow logs (who connected, and who was refused). The cost of reading flow logs is shown before anything is spent; start with 1 day. Then the Risk overview and Over-provisioning tabs compare them all, and Remediate opens one.

Security groups

Tighten an account at a time. The flow logs say what each inbound rule should become: narrower sources, fewer ports, or removed when nothing used it. One change request carries the whole plan, and a second person approves it. Open a group only to look closer.

Assets (network interfaces) and their groups

Every interface, what it is attached to and the groups it carries; every change to them is in the audit trail. To tighten one asset on its own, sg_db.py asset-plan --eni … --approved approved.json gives it a group of its own from its own flows; propose it with sg_db.py asset-propose, and it is approved and applied here. Auto scaling, EKS, ECS and AWS-managed interfaces inherit their groups: tighten the groups instead.

Remediate an account

Pick an account. Its flow logs over the look back decide what every security group should let in: narrower sources, fewer ports, unused rules removed, and groups that end up letting in the same traffic merged into one. Untick anything sensitive, then propose. The approver only approves. If something breaks after it is applied, it shows on Rejected traffic, where Propose allowing opens it up again.

Over-provisioning: every account, every asset

How many IP addresses the security groups let reach each asset that were never seen in the flow logs, rated red, amber or green, by account, and whether it is coming down. Remediate works out the new rules for that account and proposes them for approval.

Over-provisioning over time

IP addresses the security groups let in that never sent a thing in the flow logs, every account added up, at the end of each period. Falling is the goal; a step up is a rule widened or a new group.
