Security Group Console

Every account in the database

The database is the source of truth for security groups: every group and rule, as wanted and as AWS last held it. Changes are change requests: one person proposes, a different person approves, then they are applied. Changes made in AWS outside the tool are found by Reconcile. An account in surveillance mode is only reported on: what to change, and change requests, but nothing is applied. In enforce mode approved changes can be applied, once a permission check (DryRun, nothing changes) shows this identity can make them.

AWS sign-in

How do you sign in?

Signs in through your organisation's AWS access portal (IAM Identity Center), the same as aws sso login. You approve it on AWS's own page; the sign-in stays on this computer. Nothing is changed in AWS: the console reads until two people approve a change in an account switched to enforce mode.

Uses a profile or access key in ~/.aws on this computer: it acts as that identity. In other accounts it assumes one role you name (OrganizationAccountAccessRole unless you choose another); the organisation's accounts and OUs are read with the profile, so use the management account or a delegated administrator to see them all. Keys never leave this computer.

Read your accounts

Reading an account puts its security groups into the console and reads its VPC flow logs (who connected, and who was refused). The cost of reading flow logs is shown before anything is spent; start with 1 day. Then the Risk overview and Over-provisioning tabs compare them all, and Review and tighten opens one.

Assets (network interfaces) and their groups

Every interface, what it is attached to and the groups it carries; every change to them is in the audit trail. To tighten one asset on its own, sg_db.py asset-plan --eni … --approved approved.json gives it a group of its own from its own flows; propose it with sg_db.py asset-propose, and it is approved and applied here. Auto scaling, EKS, ECS and AWS-managed interfaces inherit their groups: tighten the groups instead.

Risk overview: every account

Every account sg_review.py has read, side by side: how much the security groups let in that nobody uses, whose addresses those are, what is open to the internet, who is probing, and what got in that should not have. Select an account to open its review.

Risk by account

Worst first. Red: act now. Orange: high. Amber: worth a look. Green: nothing found. The risk score adds the levels across the row.

Over-provisioned by owner

Addresses the rules let in today that were never seen in the flow logs, by who they belong to. Pull back the biggest owner first: private space nobody uses and whole provider ranges are usually most of it.
